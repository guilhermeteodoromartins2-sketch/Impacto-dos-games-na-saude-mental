A video game can be a cause of concern for a family, or it can be the tool that helps someone get through grief. Games occupy this contradictory space: they are accused of causing isolation and addiction, but are also used in therapy and in the cognitive and emotional development of those who play. On this site, we bring these two sides together: the risks and the benefits that games can bring.

Negative Points

Addiction is one of the main problems associated with video games, especially online ones, which typically feature fast-paced matches and a lot of on-screen information , making them an easy source of dopamine. Some games feature loot boxes, which exacerbate this compulsion because they involve gambling and real money in the vast majority of cases.

Stemming from this scenario, we have a sedentary lifestyle, which facilitates obesity due to the lack of physical activity and worsens various other health issues—such as poor posture, pain, and, in some cases, dry eye syndrome caused by screen brightness, as well as hearing damage from high headset volume.

Another issue that excessive screen light and overuse can lead to is sleep disruption, primarily caused by playing late into the night. The blue light produced by screens significantly impairs sleep, affecting its quality, cognitive performance, and even leading to irritability..

Spending long hours sitting and doing nothing but playing can lead to a certain digital dependence, causing the individual to isolate themselves from the real world and live only in the virtual one, which can result in social anxiety, depression, and other issues.

Positive points

The therapeutic use of games can be surprising, given that screens are frequently blamed for certain problems . However, studies like the one from the JAMA Network have shown that when used correctly, games can assist in the treatment of depression and other emotional conditions. In the study, an educational game called PlaySmart was tested on 532 high school students. However, it is important to note that not every game is suitable for therapeutic purposes; another study using a different program showed a reduction in the stigma surrounding mental health, but only a modest reduction in depressive symptoms.

In addition to aiding mental health, games can also foster cognitive and social development. Researchers from UFSC studied this precise topic, observing over 4 weeks that cognitive games helped children (ages 8–11) improve self-control, attention, and problem-solving skills. In another study conducted by a group of psychologists, it was shown that games like RPGs can be used to develop socio-emotional skills in children and adolescents, while also improving executive functions—a deficit present in ADHD—and encouraging participation in sports, which is crucial in treating anxiety.

Some games also offer emotional representation and understanding. One of them is Celeste, an indie platformer where Madeline, the protagonist, decides to climb Mount Celeste. Throughout her journey, she faces an "evil" version of herself—Badeline—who is nothing more than a representation of her insecurity, trying to demotivate her along the way and force her to give up, serving as a clear metaphor for anxiety and self-sabotage. Her journey up the mountain is a struggle for her mental health and the acceptance of who she truly is. There are several other games like this, and on the next page, we will dive deeper into them.
