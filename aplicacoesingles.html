Cognitive Developmenth

Several scientific reviews show that playing video games on a regular basis is associated with measurable gains in specific cognitive functions, working memory, selective attention, and inhibitory control—a set of skills that neuropsychology calls executive functions. Brazilian research, such as that conducted at UFSC's Laboratory for Research and Outreach in Cognitive Games (LabLudens), specifically investigates how these games can be used to stimulate inhibitory control and emotional competence in children

Leisure and Emotional Regulation

Just like Celeste, Gris is an indie game that elevates the gaming medium to an incredible artistic level. One of the most popular interpretations is that Gris is about grief and its stages, based on the 5-stage model proposed by psychiatrist Elisabeth Kübler-Ross (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance); however, this model is considered outdated and is not widely used in current research in the field. In this game, there are no dialogues, only colors, and with each color added, a new stage of the grieving process is expressed through various metaphors. For instance, during certain moments of gameplay, a shapeless black mass pursues the player, representing sadness, anger, or any other negative feeling that haunts us during grief.

Education

Gamification applies typical game elements—points, levels, progressive challenges, and rewards—to educational contexts, shifting motivation from a final grade to the satisfaction of overcoming a challenge. According to researcher Karl Kapp, this approach is particularly effective in subjects like science and programming, where solving problems and experimenting with solutions are already part of the natural learning process. Research on the topic shows consistent results in engagement and content retention across various educational levels, which has been driving the popularity of this format in Brazilian classrooms.
